The operating system that improves itself.

BeeCodersOs is a 64-bit OS written from scratch in Rust. It notices what goes wrong and what people miss, and an AI turns that into small, tested changes that a person approves. Every new kernel boots on trial and rolls back by itself if it fails.

Mission

Software that gets better because it's used, not because someone files a bug.

Today, an operating system improves only when a person notices a problem, writes it down, and someone else finds the time to fix it. Most of what goes wrong is never reported.

BeeCodersOs closes that gap. The system keeps track of its own crashes, the commands people reach for that don't exist, and the wishes they type in. That becomes a backlog, and the backlog becomes code.

AI does the legwork, but the system stays careful: every change is small, tested and approved by a person, and the system can always go back to the version that worked.

The improvement loop

Five steps, each one small enough to check and to undo.

  1. It observes

    Crashes, failed commands, commands that don't exist and wishes are recorded on the machine. It records command names only, never what you typed after them.

  2. It collects

    beehive, a small daemon on a host computer, gathers those signals from every machine and turns them into a backlog.

  3. An AI writes the change

    A coding agent takes one backlog item, writes the change and a test for it, and has to pass the whole test suite.

  4. A person approves

    Nothing lands on its own. A human reviews every change before it is merged.

  5. It tries it safely

    The new kernel goes into the spare of two boot slots and boots on trial. If it fails its health check, the machine goes back to the old kernel by itself.

What it can do today

A real system, on a real machine.

Everything below runs on an HP EliteDesk with four cores, booted from its own SATA disk. These screenshots are real; none of them are mock-ups.

AI

Talk to it

ask answers questions about the system, explain explains the last error or crash, and do turns a task in plain words into shell commands that run only after you agree.

Network

Its own network stack

Ethernet drivers, ARP, IPv4, DHCP, DNS, TCP, HTTP and HTTPS, time and file transfer, all written from scratch, down to the cryptography. Every byte it receives is treated as untrusted.

Storage

Its own file system

BeeFS works on RAM, IDE and SATA disks. There's an installer that puts the OS on a disk, and kernel updates arrive over the network.

Windows

Windows that tile themselves

desktop tile on lays the windows out side by side as on Omarchy: the first takes the left half, the next the top of what is left, and so on, with gaps between them. Super+arrows move between them, Super+Shift+arrows swap them, Super+1–9 switch workspaces, Super+Space opens a launcher, Super+K lists the keys, Super+F fills the screen. Floating, a window is dragged by its title bar, resized by its corner and folded up to its title bar; long lists and terminals get a scroll bar, and terminals keep 1000 lines to look back on. All the keys.

Desktop

Windows, mouse, files with icons

Windows you can drag, resize by their corner and fold up to their title bar, scroll with a bar, or let tile themselves side by side as on Omarchy (desktop tile on, with its Super keys to move between and swap them), a USB mouse, buttons that start programs, several terminals, a text editor, and a graphical file manager: icons for folders, text and sounds, and real thumbnails of pictures, decoded on every processor at once. Enter opens a picture in the viewer and plays a sound; c, x and p copy and move files. The editor finds (Ctrl+F), undoes (Ctrl+Z), cuts and pastes (Ctrl+K, Ctrl+U).

Web

A web browser, with pictures

browse shows web pages as clean text with their pictures in place (JPEG, PNG, GIF, decoded by its own code and made to fit): headings, lists, and links you follow with Tab or a click. Like a reader view it starts at the page's main part, without menus and hidden controls (m shows everything). Type words instead of an address and it searches the web. HTTPS works through its own TLS 1.3 and its own cryptography, and it checks RSA and ECDSA certificates all the way to 118 trusted roots: Wikipedia checks out in 0.7 s on the HP.

Video

It plays videos

play honey.avi shows a Motion-JPEG video in a window, every frame decoded on all four cores and shown at its time, with its sound on the sound card. Try it: this demo video (3.2 MB) plays on the HP after a wget https://os.beecoders.com/media/honey.avi. And record screen.avi 10 films the desktop into a video of its own, ten JPEG frames a second made on every core.

Camera

It sees you

Plug in a USB webcam: its own USB Video Class driver agrees on a size with the camera, streams 8000 small packets a second over the USB controller and puts them back together into pictures. camera view shows it live in a window, like a mirror; camera snap me.jpg takes a photo; camera record hello.avi 10 films a video you can play. And camera watch photos makes it a security camera: it saves a picture whenever something moves, but not when the light changes; camera timelapse plant.avi 60 turns a whole day into under three minutes of video. Works on the HP up to 1280×720.

Sound

It can play music

Its own HD Audio driver finds the speaker and jacks: beep c5 plays a note, tune c5 e5 g5 c6 a melody (or saves it as a WAV file), play song.wav a WAV file, timer 10m tea rings when the tea is ready and alarm 07:30 when it is time to get up. To make the HP's speaker play it had to switch off a channel the firmware enabled on one side of the chipset only.

Time

A clock that knows the season

The desktop shows the local date and time. timezone CET follows Europe's summer time by itself; date shows local time with UTC beside it.

Multi-core

Every core at work

All processors start at boot, and work is split across them. Hashing 64 MiB takes 130 ms instead of 521 ms on four cores. A screenshot is compressed in 16 pieces at once: the HP's whole screen as a 53 KB PNG in 30 ms.

Remote

Driven from afar

Any VNC viewer can see and use its screen (vnc on, with a password the OS makes up). From the host you can run commands and deploy a new kernel over the network; the machine reports whether it is healthy.

Pictures

Pictures, animations and screen recording

screenshot saves the screen as PNG or GIF, view shows JPEG photos (baseline and progressive), PNG and GIF pictures (decoded by its own JPEG, DEFLATE, PNG and LZW code; a 3-megapixel photo in 0.44 s, its blocks spread over every core) and plays animated GIFs, and record clip.gif 5 films the desktop: five pictures a second, only what changed is kept, so five seconds of the HP's 1280×1024 screen fit in 35 KB. In the viewer the arrow keys go through the folder's pictures, and s turns them into a slideshow. desktop wallpaper puts any picture behind everything, scaled on every core. Saving a 3.8 MB picture takes under a second: 15× faster since the file system stopped rewriting its bookkeeping for every block.

Careful by design

An AI may write the code. It can't break your machine.

⟲

A/B kernel slots

A new kernel boots on trial. If it doesn't pass its health check, the machine goes back to the last good one without anyone touching it.

✓

Tests before anything ships

make check boots the system dozens of times in virtual machines (BIOS and UEFI, disks, network, desktop, crashes and rollbacks), with no internet and no AI.

◉

Privacy first

The event log keeps command names, never what you typed after them. Host names and addresses are never logged. API keys stay on the host.

⏱

A watchdog

If a program ever keeps the processor and starves the rest, the timer notices within 30 seconds, records what happened and restarts the machine. Learnt the hard way: a video player froze the HP until someone pressed its power button.

⏻

An off switch

ai off stops every AI feature, and the system works fine without them. Nothing listens on the network unless you switch it on.
